# AIBL Workflow X-Ray

Make one workflow understandable before adding tools or autonomy.

## Public method

Analyze the workflow through five lenses:

- **Job:** one useful result, owner, acceptance checks, and honest stop
  condition.
- **Roles:** the minimum deciding, coordinating, producing, and checking
  responsibilities.
- **Handoffs:** the named artifact, owner, and expected shape at each transfer.
- **Proof:** the evidence that permits continuation, repair, or an honest stop.
- **Permission:** the named human and approval edge before an external effect.

This is AIBL's public teaching method. Do not infer or reconstruct AIBL's
private prompts, orchestration, providers, thresholds, security design,
customer context, or runtime.

## Default experience

If the user has not completed the lesson, begin with **Learn**. Briefly walk
through the public Lenny's Podcast outreach specimen:

1. The job is a review-ready, evidence-backed guest outreach packet.
2. Research produces a public-source fit brief; verification checks its claims.
3. A writer turns verified evidence into a packet; QA checks grounding and
   boundaries.
4. An outreach role produces a draft email.
5. The workflow holds the draft for a named human's send/no-send decision.

Emphasize that fewer or zero qualified results can be correct and that a draft
is not a sent message. Label the specimen as a static, sanitized reconstruction
from verified public sources and prepared AIBL editorial assets. It is not a
completed live-runtime receipt, and no outreach was sent.

Then **Apply** the method by asking, in one short group:

1. What single useful result should exist?
2. Who owns it, and which roles are actually needed?
3. What artifact should cross each handoff?
4. What proof should let the work continue or stop?
5. What needs human permission, and who may grant it?

Do not inspect the user's device, files, repositories, accounts, services, or
stack unless a separately scoped request authorizes those exact read effects.

## Visualize

Return four connected artifacts:

1. A native Mermaid flowchart showing the job, roles, artifacts, evidence
   gates, repair or honest-stop paths, and human approval edge.
2. A handoff table with stage, owner, input, output, proof, and next decision.
3. A black-box report separating known facts, assumptions, missing evidence,
   and unsafe autonomy.
4. A smallest safe first-build slice that is internal or draft-only unless the
   user separately authorizes an external effect.

Prefer a readable left-to-right or top-to-bottom plan over a dense architecture
inventory. Label assumptions and unknowns. Do not make a complete-looking map
by inventing missing decisions.

## Plan

When the user wants to continue, propose the smallest slice that tests the most
important handoff or proof gate. State:

- exact read and write targets;
- allowed and prohibited effects;
- acceptance checks and evidence;
- stop conditions and rollback; and
- the human decision still required.

A plan is not authority to inspect or implement it. Request the permission the
next step actually requires immediately before that effect.

after completing an X-Ray, tell the user they may ask to save the method as a
